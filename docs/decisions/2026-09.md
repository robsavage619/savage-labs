## 2026-09-05: The chart follows the data's shape, and an impossible reading is shown, not dropped

**Context.** Rob opened the app and said things didn't fit. Three separate causes. (1) Three bar charts were rendering *nothing*, recharts' bar animation does not complete under React 19, so a `<Bar>` missing `isAnimationActive={false}` emits an empty `<g>` per datum with correct axes and correct tooltips around it. (2) Several charts used a mark the data cannot support: the Recent Labs "Trend" column was a sparkline over histories that are **one point deep for 46 of 52 labs**, so 88% of it drew an em-dash; body weight plotted 117 weigh-ins spread across nine years as bars-to-the-axis on an *ordinal* x, producing a grey slab on a falsified timeline; the mesocycle form chart drew TSB (~−1) and CTL (~10) against one hidden auto domain, so CTL set the scale and flattened form into a straight line with its own ±5/−10 guides off-screen. (3) `/week` carried ~3,400px and `/lab` ~1,650px of empty right-hand column, from cards left at `span-2` with nothing beside them.

**Decision.** Charts are chosen from what the data can actually answer. The labs column becomes a **reference-range position track**, where a value sits inside its own interval, which a single draw can answer and a sparkline cannot. Body weight gets a real time axis with the readings as points and the rolling mean as the line. Form gets its own axis and its threshold bands. Both scatter axes get explicit ticks carrying their unit, after recharts' auto-ticks produced "2.12 / 5.12 / 10.26" for an hours axis. On layout: **a card with no partner takes the full width**, and two short cards stack into one shared column, the corollary the week page's own doc comment had stated in one direction only.

Separately: two 138 lb entries sit inside a 233-239 lb run (2026-05-19/21, a 100 lb round trip in five days). They are excluded from the trend line and from the y-domain, but **drawn**, hollow, at the domain edge, and counted in a note under the chart naming the rule that caught them.

**Why.** A mark that cannot be read is worse than no mark: the blank bars and the em-dash column both cost board space while answering nothing, and the flattened form chart actively misled by drawing a real signal as a flat line. On the weight outliers, "fail visibly, not silently" (CLAUDE.md) cuts against a quiet filter, the alternative of leaving them in was not neutral either, since they dragged the axis down by a hundred pounds and gouged the rolling mean, hiding nine years of real trend to display two bad rows. Showing them at the edge with a stated reason keeps the reading honest in both directions.

**Consequences.** The exclusion threshold is deliberately crude, >25% from the median of the six surrounding weigh-ins, which cannot fire on any physiological change at this sampling density. It is a **display** guard, not a fix: the contaminated rows are still in the source and should be corrected at ingest, which is why the note says so on the page rather than hiding the count. `isAnimationActive={false}` is now required on every recharts series in the app; the failure is silent and intermittent (a re-render can fill the bars in), so it is verified by counting empty `.recharts-bar-rectangle` groups in the DOM rather than by looking. Two multi-column blocks lost a negative bottom margin that was landing their legend on top of the longest column. Text collisions are checked by walking leaf rects, not by reading screenshots, the Browser pane renders a 1440px viewport at ~0.55 scale, where correct 10px type looks broken and a real 16px overlap does not. See [[feedback_recharts_react19_blank_bars]] and [[feedback_shc_board_layout]].

## 2026-09-05: `shc seed` wrote 90 fabricated nights into the live database

**Context.** `sleep` held 1,104 rows across 996 distinct nights and `recovery` 1,007 rows across 915 dates. The extras were not WHOOP revisions, they were `shc seed` output: 90 nights each, ids `seed_rec_2026-01-21` … `seed_sleep_2026-04-20`, `content_hash = 'seed'`, and crucially **`source = 'whoop'`**, which made them indistinguishable from real rows to every query that did not also filter on the hash. The values are `random.gauss(65, 12)` HRV and `random.gauss(72, 15)` recovery: over that window the fabricated HRV averaged 65.9 ms against a real 114.7 ms, pulling the window mean to 90.3. Every all-time statistic on the Week board, the 1,007-day recovery distribution, avg-recovery-by-day-of-week, was computed over a sample that was 9% invented. The duplication also silently multiplied any query joining `sleep` to `recovery`: a 180-day window returned 308 rows.

**Decision.** Delete the 180 seeded rows, all 90 dates in both tables were verified to also carry a real row, so nothing was lost, and make the command refuse to run against a database that already holds non-seed recovery rows, with `--force` as the deliberate override. A locked or absent database counts as "nothing to protect" and is waved through, because `_seed` needs the same write lock and fails on it a moment later without writing. The endpoint that surfaced this now picks one row per night explicitly (longest session per `night_date`, one recovery per date) rather than trusting `is_nap`, which is unreliable: 2026-08-25 carries a 1.1h afternoon session flagged as a night.

**Why.** "Fail visibly, not silently" (CLAUDE.md) is the standard, and synthetic data tagged with a real source name is the exact opposite, it cannot be told from the truth at the point of use, only at the point of writing. A guard at the writer is the only place the distinction is still knowable. Deleting rather than relabelling is right because every seeded date is a duplicate: there is no night whose only record is synthetic, so no gap is opened.

**Consequences.** All-time recovery statistics change, the distribution drops from 1,007 to 917 scored days and the Jan, Apr 2026 HRV mean corrects from 90.3 to 114.7 ms. Anything previously fitted over that window (personal HRV bands, sleep bands, engine accuracy history) was fitted on contaminated data and should be re-fitted before its output is trusted. The seeder was additionally broken, it inserted a `sleep.rhr` column dropped from the schema, so it has been failing outright for some time; fixed, since a guarded command may as well work. Sixteen further duplicate nights remain in `sleep` and two in `recovery`: those are genuine multi-session days and mislabelled naps, not contamination, and are handled at read time rather than deleted. Coverage: `backend/tests/test_sleep_airway.py`.
