## 2026-11-24: A single-arm set counts half, and the two arms stay two lifts

**Context.** The left-against-right biceps experiment logs every direct biceps set as `Single Arm Dumbbell Curl (Left)` or `(Right)`. The engine knew neither name, and every counter takes one set as one set, so left 8 plus right 14 would read as 22 biceps sets against a budget of 8. Separately, `canon_exercise` stripped any trailing parenthesis, so both arms collapsed into one lift for the lab's session e1RM and the dashboard rollups.

**Decision.** Migration 0123 copies the `Seated Dumbbell Curl` rows (biceps primary, head and citation) onto both names. `load_mechanics.set_weight` returns 0.5 for a `(Left)`/`(Right)` suffix and 1.0 otherwise; every counter of credited or direct sets multiplies by it (listed in invariant 27). The weight is a name rule in code rather than a column, because DuckDB refuses to alter a table that views depend on. The same migration redefines `workout_sets_dedup` so a side tag is kept in `canon_exercise`.

**Why.** An arm's set stimulates one arm, so a pair of single-arm sets equals one bilateral set; left 8 plus right 14 is 11 per arm-pair. Per-hand load needed no change: the logged weight is already the one dumbbell, the identity.

**Consequences.** The rule applies to any future side-tagged exercise, which is the intent. Rows logged before the exercises existed do not exist, so no history moves. `research.py` and `dashboard.py` counts that join `exercise_muscle` themselves (`research.py` lines near 463 and 485, `dashboard.py` near 1949) still count a single-arm set as 1.0 and need the same weight. Tests: `test_single_arm_curls.py`.

## 2026-11-24: One meaning for a direct glute set, and the planner is told

**Context.** A review of migration 0122 asked whether every counter of DIRECT (primary-role) sets applies the abduction rule. The ten `role = 'primary'` reads in `backend/src/shc` and the primary-role counts in validators #18, #22 and #23 were audited.

**Decision.** Only counters that measure direct work toward a floor or budget use `mesocycle.DIRECT_SET_SQL`: `direct_sets_by_week` (maintenance delivery, deload thin-work exclusion), `weekly_budget_block`, and the direct floor in `weekly_prescription`. All already did; none needed switching. The rest stay on primary role because they classify, gate rest, define menus or count credited volume (list in invariant 26). The glutes budget line now says hip abduction trains glute medius and does not count toward it.

**Why.** The counters were consistent, but the planner saw "glutes: 0/6 left" next to a menu full of abductors and had no reason to pick a hip thrust. One line in the context closes that.

**Consequences.** Validator #22 still counts abduction toward a held glutes cap, so an abduction-heavy session can be rejected while the floor reads zero direct work; kept on purpose so a recovery hold cannot be bypassed with abduction. Test: `test_budget_line_tells_the_planner_abduction_is_not_glute_max`, `test_abduction_only_week_still_owes_direct_glute_sets`.

## 2026-11-24: Abduction no longer counts as direct glute-max work

**Context.** The hypertrophy specialist found 41 of 49 glute-primary sets since August were machine hip abduction, with no hip thrust since 2026-09-05 and no RDL since 2026-07-26. The engine has one `glutes` muscle, so abduction was primary 1.0, the same as a hip thrust, and satisfied every direct-set counter. Gluteus maximus, the muscle the emphasis means, was fed by about 4 spillover sets a week.

**Decision.** Keep abduction as primary glutes credit but stop counting it as a DIRECT set. Migration 0122 tags the three untagged abductors `gluteus_medius`, and `mesocycle.DIRECT_SET_SQL` excludes that region from the weekly direct-set budget, the direct-work floor and the maintenance delivery counts (invariant 26). Validator #22's cap and #23's emphasis lower bound are unchanged, so an abduction-only plan still counts as trained. The first proposal, demoting the abductor rows to secondary 0.5, was rejected: `SECONDARY_CREDIT` is 1.0 so no credit would change, and each abductor has one row, which would leave a NULL `primary_muscle` in the `exercise_muscle_map` view and remove the lifts from every menu keyed on it. The vault (`schoenfeld-2021-ch8-program-design.md`) wants abduction kept for glute med/min, so it is reclassified, not penalised.

**Why.** The vault says abduction and hip extension train different parts of the glutes and neither substitutes for the other. One shared label let the easier lift fill the quota of the harder one.

**Consequences.** The glutes budget line (6 sets) and floor now need hip thrust, bridge, deadlift or kickback sets; abduction is extra. Abduction still raises the credited total, so the ceiling and the weekly volume number do not drop. Other glute rows without a head tag still count as direct, and a new abduction variant needs its own tagging migration. Nothing reaches the live database until the next deploy.

## 2026-11-23: The lab cannot answer what it asks, so registration now refuses such studies

**Context.** An audit of the lab two weeks after the research layer went live found sixteen registered questions, none answered. The three still active put their own confirmation waits at roughly 470, 1,200 and 4,200 more days, and the older trial portfolio ran from about 900 to 24,000 days, or had no estimate at all. One randomized trial had been assigned in the planner for months with no data points, because its outcome exercise name matched no logged exercise: its outcome was never measured.

**Decision.** `selflab.preregister` raises `ValueError` (353d40f) when the outcome exercise has no logged working set in the last 60 days under its exact name, or when a randomized study's days to answer exceeds 120 at the outcome's live noise, its recent pace and 80% adherence. Observational designs skip the second check: their arms are observed, not assigned, so the outcome's measurement rate is not their accrual pace. The 120-day limit mirrors `research.MAX_DAYS_TO_ANSWER`, duplicated because `research` imports `selflab`.

**Why.** A rule that lives in a prompt or a review note relies on someone remembering it; a refusal at registration cannot be skipped. Registering a question that cannot resolve within a season costs nothing at the time and fills the queue with studies that will never inform a decision.

**Consequences.** Existing studies are not retroactively refused; the gate only stops new ones. Studies that were already unanswerable still need closing or pausing by hand.

## 2026-11-23: The planner prompt shows only confirmed, refuted and error lab verdicts

**Context.** The personal-lab block of the planner prompt told the model the findings "override population assumptions" and listed every check, including inconclusive and insufficient ones. The old fixed-runner catalogue is unstable: over six months it produced eight confirmations, and every one has since reverted to refuted, insufficient or inconclusive.

**Decision.** The block carries only confirmed, refuted and error rows (318d2cf), and the wording now says to weigh them alongside population evidence instead of overriding it. Error rows stay because a crashed runner must remain visible rather than vanish from the prompt.

**Why.** An inconclusive row says nothing, and an unstable verdict presented as an override teaches the model to trust the layer that has been wrong most often. Showing silence as absence is accurate; showing a crash as absence is not.

**Consequences.** The prompt is shorter and the model sees fewer lab claims. The old fixed-runner catalogue still feeds the block; whether to retire it is a separate decision.

## 2026-11-23: A missing vault is reported, not skipped

**Context.** The vault lives outside the repo, on the host's disk. On the live host it was absent, so the served planner context had no vault catalog or excerpts, the vault endpoints returned empty lists, and the citation check switched itself off, with nothing on any screen saying so.

**Decision.** `vault_context` returns an explicit `VAULT UNAVAILABLE` notice instead of an empty string, telling the model it is ungrounded and must cite nothing (39178f6). `/api/freshness` gains a Vault row, state `missing` when the index reads no notes, and the sync chip lists it in its own wording.

**Why.** A silent skip is indistinguishable from a working system until someone reads the prompt. The same rule applies to every gated input: if the data is absent, say so where the consumer and the user can both see it.

**Consequences.** Citation validation still skips when its allow-list is empty, so the notice is the only signal on that path. The Vault row appears only on hosts running a build that includes this commit.

## 2026-11-23: Standard errors use Newey-West because the lag-1 correction reads 1.0 on sparse rows

**Context.** `estimate()` widened its interval by a design effect built from lag-1 autocorrelation across calendar-consecutive days, floored at zero. For questions whose unit is a training session, consecutive-day pairs are rare, so the measure returned exactly 1.0 and clustered errors never widened anything, even where re-estimating from the data implied an effect two to three times larger.

**Decision.** The arm coefficient's variance is now a Newey-West estimate over rows in observation order, with lag max(10, n^(1/3)) capped at n/4 (073f3c6). `design_effect` is reported as the ratio of the robust to the classical variance, floored at 1.0.

**Why.** The calendar-lag shortcut assumed one row per day. Ordering by observation removes that assumption, and a heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent variance needs no model of the correlation, which is the safer default when the residual structure is unknown.

**Consequences.** Intervals widen and waits lengthen, which is the correct direction and will retire more questions. Nothing else changed: the forecast scorer and the early-look rule are separate decisions.

## 2026-11-23: The lab job definition lives in deploy/

**Context.** The lab prompt, its conf and its launchd plist existed only on the host, and the 2026-11-12 to 2026-11-17 outage, where the job runner could not find its command, was silent: a run that did nothing exited cleanly and raised no alert.

**Decision.** `deploy/jobs/lab.md`, `deploy/jobs/lab.conf` and `deploy/com.savage.shc-lab.plist` are committed (06f0263), and `deploy/run-job.sh` posts a start and end note to the lab notebook, best effort: a failed post changes neither the job nor its exit code. `sync-claude-copies.sh` installs job files only where the host lacks them, with a `--force-jobs` override; `imac.sh` does not install the lab plist.

**Why.** Configuration that exists on one machine cannot be reviewed, diffed or rebuilt. A notebook heartbeat turns "ran and did nothing" into a visible gap.

**Consequences.** The committed files are a reconstruction from the specialist agent definitions and the documented cycle, not copies of the host's files; the prompt wording, tool and turn limits and the schedule are drafted placeholders. Diff them against the host's copies before overwriting either.

## 2026-11-14: Progress photos removed

**Context.** The photo feature measured silhouette widths from a front and a side shot and reported waist:shoulder and waist:hip ratios, gated at a 2% noise floor. Those ratios fed a body-composition block in DailyState, the briefing and planner context, the daily report, and a bounded "physique" volume nudge in autoregulation. A follow-up trial fitted a 3D body model (MHR) to the two photos to read tape-style girths.

**Result.** Against same-morning tape, three front/side pairs read the waist 14 cm high and the mid-thigh 8 cm low while agreeing with each other within 2%. Two relaxed photos cannot see thigh depth (the far leg sits behind the near one), chest depth (the hanging arm) or a flexed arm, so any model fills those in from an average body. Only one photo session had ever been stored, so the physique nudge had never fired and the ratio trend had never existed.

**Decision.** Remove the feature: the upload, analysis and critique routes, the vision package, the Body-page panel, the photo row in the readings ledger and growth-review cadence, the scorecard count, the DailyState body-composition block and its consumers, the physique input to `_resolve_emphasis`, and the image-analysis dependencies. The trial folder goes too; its verdict is in git history (70342c6). Tape is the girth instrument.

**Why.** A measurement that cannot see two of the three growth-review sites, and reads the third worse than a tape measure, adds code, a 176 MB model download and a dependency pinned to the Intel host without adding information.

**Consequences.** No prescription changes: the physique nudge returned nothing on the stored data, so emphasis was already Rob's stored priorities or the biceps/glutes prior. At Rob's request the data went too: migration 0121 drops the `progress_photos`, `photo_measurements` and `physique_critiques` tables, and the stored photo files were deleted. The 2026-11-13 entry below is history.

## 2026-11-13: Photo analysis runs on OpenCV and rembg, no MediaPipe package

**Context.** Since the move to the Intel host on 2026-11-11 the photo upload returned 503 there: `mediapipe` and `rembg` were excluded on Intel Macs because neither they nor the locked `onnxruntime` shipped wheels for it. The 2026-11-01 audit had also flagged that a retake which fails analysis deletes the photo already stored for that date, and that the upload route took no key.

**Result.** PyPI's file lists show `onnxruntime` 1.23.2 and `numba` 0.62.1 are the last releases with Intel-Mac wheels (Python 3.12 and 3.13), and `mediapipe` 0.10.21 the last, for 3.12 only. The `mediapipe` runtime also aborts the whole process when it cannot reach a Metal GPU service. `backend/scripts/vision_stability.py` measured the pipeline on the 2026-05-25 photos under 13 capture-like perturbations: the u2net silhouette is stable (0.16% mean outline movement) and a larger model does not improve on it; the MediaPipe Pose network in OpenCV Zoo's ONNX export, run through `cv2.dnn`, holds waist:shoulder to 0.52% (front) and 0.57% (side) and reproduces the stored ratios within 1.5%. Widths scaled by the trunk span moved about 5% from re-framing alone.

**Decision.** Drop the `mediapipe` dependency and run the pose network through OpenCV. Install `rembg` everywhere, with `onnxruntime<1.24` and `numba<0.63` on Intel Macs. Change verdicts cover the two ratios only. The upload analyzes a staged copy and replaces the stored photo only on success, runs off the event loop, and takes the admin key like the other mutating routes. A new image route serves stored photos, optionally registered onto another date's frame, for the session gallery and the before/after slider.

**Why.** The host is the only place the app runs, so a pipeline that cannot install there measures nothing. The pose model is the same family as before, which keeps the visibility thresholds and the stored baseline valid. A 2% gate on a metric with 5% framing noise reports the camera, not the body.

**Consequences.** `onnxruntime` and `numba` are frozen on the Intel host at their last compatible releases; when `rembg` raises its floor past 1.23.2 the host needs a different silhouette runtime. The first upload on the host downloads the pose model (5.5 MB, hash-checked) and u2net (176 MB). The Intel install was resolved against PyPI for that platform but not run on the host. Re-upload the 2026-05-25 pair so its readings come from the new pose path. Numbers and rejected alternatives: `backend/src/shc/vision/METHODOLOGY.md` §7. Tests: `test_failed_reupload_keeps_the_stored_photo`, `test_stalled_model_download_fails_instead_of_hanging`.

## 2026-11-13: Below fever range, skin temp is a note, not an illness cap

**Context.** The 2026-07-12 decision let a 0.9°F skin-temp rise cap the day to LOW and Z2-only when HRV, recovery or RHR corroborated it. It had never been scored against real sick days. A replay ran each gate as-of every morning over 800 scored days (2024-01 to 2026-05) against 9 self-reported sick episodes (WHOOP journal "Feeling sick or ill?" plus `daily_checkin.illness_flag`), with a null that dropped the same episodes on random dates 1,000 times.

**Result.** The skin-temp gate caught 4 of 9 episodes, against 4.2 expected by chance, while firing on 88 of 800 days (3.7 false alarms a month on clean days). On 18 of 23 sick days skin temp sat below its 28-day baseline. The respiratory-rate gate caught 6 of 9 against 2.5 by chance (p = 0.03), 5 of them on or before the first sick day.

**Decision.** A 0.9-2.0°F rise is reported as a note and caps nothing. A fever-range spike (2.0°F or more) still caps to LOW and Z2-only. The respiratory-rate gate and `_illness_gate_corroborated` are unchanged.

**Why.** A cap that fires on one day in nine and predicts nothing better than chance costs training days without buying safety. The independent HRV (below -1.5 sigma) and readiness caps still act on a genuinely bad day.

**Consequences.** Nine episodes, self-reported, six of them in June and July 2024, which may have been allergy rather than infection; five rules were compared, so p = 0.03 is suggestive. Journal answers are sparse after 2024-11, so re-run the replay once more sick days are logged. Tests: `test_sub_fever_skin_temp_is_a_note_not_a_cap_when_recovery_data_absent`, `test_corroborated_sub_fever_skin_temp_no_longer_forces_z2_only`, `test_skin_temp_fever_range_caps_even_on_green`.
