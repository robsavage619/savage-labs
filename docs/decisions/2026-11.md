## 2026-11-13: Below fever range, skin temp is a note, not an illness cap

**Context.** The 2026-07-12 decision let a 0.9°F skin-temp rise cap the day to LOW and Z2-only when HRV, recovery or RHR corroborated it. It had never been scored against real sick days. A replay ran each gate as-of every morning over 800 scored days (2024-01 to 2026-05) against 9 self-reported sick episodes (WHOOP journal "Feeling sick or ill?" plus `daily_checkin.illness_flag`), with a null that dropped the same episodes on random dates 1,000 times.

**Result.** The skin-temp gate caught 4 of 9 episodes, against 4.2 expected by chance, while firing on 88 of 800 days (3.7 false alarms a month on clean days). On 18 of 23 sick days skin temp sat below its 28-day baseline. The respiratory-rate gate caught 6 of 9 against 2.5 by chance (p = 0.03), 5 of them on or before the first sick day.

**Decision.** A 0.9-2.0°F rise is reported as a note and caps nothing. A fever-range spike (2.0°F or more) still caps to LOW and Z2-only. The respiratory-rate gate and `_illness_gate_corroborated` are unchanged.

**Why.** A cap that fires on one day in nine and predicts nothing better than chance costs training days without buying safety. The independent HRV (below -1.5 sigma) and readiness caps still act on a genuinely bad day.

**Consequences.** Nine episodes, self-reported, six of them in June and July 2024, which may have been allergy rather than infection; five rules were compared, so p = 0.03 is suggestive. Journal answers are sparse after 2024-11, so re-run the replay once more sick days are logged. Tests: `test_sub_fever_skin_temp_is_a_note_not_a_cap_when_recovery_data_absent`, `test_corroborated_sub_fever_skin_temp_no_longer_forces_z2_only`, `test_skin_temp_fever_range_caps_even_on_green`.
