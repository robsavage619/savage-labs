## 2026-08-20: Detraining is not fatigue, and only exposure can tell them apart

**Context.** `metrics._e1rm_regression` detects a peak-vs-peak e1RM drop over 56 days and, past −3%, sets `deload_required` directly. It carries careful guards for basis (invariant 13), units (invariant 19), deload-day contamination and RPE-coverage skew, but it had **no exposure term at all**. A lift that got weaker because it stopped being trained was indistinguishable from one that got weaker under accumulated fatigue, and those need opposite prescriptions. Live on 2026-08-20: Split Squat (Dumbbell) read −11.1% off **10 working sets in 8 weeks** (1.2/wk, with a 22-day gap) while resistance ACWR sat at **0.42** and 8-week volume was down 19.6%. The engine's answer to "you stopped training this" was to prescribe less training; only the 9-day cooldown being coincidentally live prevented it. Rob raised it as "if a workout didn't happen, I don't want to be punished, it was a personal decision to rest."

**Decision.** Add `_regression_cause`, which qualifies a detected regression as `detrain` or `overreach` before the gate acts on it, and branch in `_gates` so the detraining case surfaces a re-accumulation reason instead of `deload_required`. Two independent vault-backed tests, either sufficient: average working sets/week below **MV** (`_MV_SETS_PER_WEEK` = 2.0) or a **≥14-day zero-exposure gap** (`_DETRAIN_GAP_DAYS`), the tail-to-today stretch included. Exposure counts the same rows the detector scores, working sets only, deload days excluded, so a set the detector ignored cannot count as training. `AutoRegGates.e1rm_regression_cause` is new and optional; the cause is computed at the call site alongside `_deload_in_cooldown` rather than inside `_gates`, matching how `e1rm_pct`/`e1rm_lift` already reach it. Recorded as invariant 21.

**Why.** Directly invariant 10, under-training must be reachable "only by an explicit, stated intent, never by absence, typo, or inference", and a deload fired by absence is exactly inference. The direction is the real safety property: `overreach` is the pre-existing status quo, so the classifier can only ever *withhold* a deload, never impose one; a bug leaves Rob training rather than resting him by accident. Thresholds are vault-sourced, not invented: MV = 1-2 hard sets/muscle/week (`volume-landmarks-mev-mav-mrv.md`, top of range taken deliberately because it errs toward training him), and detraining onset at 2 weeks of zero exposure (`periodization-strength-phases.md`, `bompa-2021-ch6-annual-plan.md`). The vault contradicts itself on onset, the same author says 4 weeks elsewhere in `bompa-2021-ch6-annual-plan.md`, so 2 weeks was **chosen, not averaged**: it is the earlier trigger, and earlier here means suppressing a deload. Two further vault findings shape it: `overreaching-detection.md` ranks 1RM the **last and least sensitive** overreaching marker (psychological -> sprint velocity -> bar velocity -> eccentric RFD -> 1RM), so an isolated e1RM drop is weak fatigue evidence; and `age-related-hypertrophy.md` puts the maintenance dose **higher** at 40, making absence the more likely cause rather than less.

**Consequences.** `deload_required` semantics are untouched, so none of its ~15 consumers change, they simply stop seeing a deload that absence explains. The planner's CONSTRAINT line now carries the cause, and the "is this overreaching, and how to respond?" uncertainty at `workout_planner.py` is suppressed when the answer is known, so the vault is not asked to re-litigate a resolved question toward the opposite prescription. `test_compute_daily_state.py::test_recent_deload_plan_suppresses_via_pipeline` needed its **fixture** widened (not its assertion weakened): its four-session/8-set history was itself a detraining profile, so it no longer reached the cooldown branch it exists to test; it now trains every 4 days, 7 heavy + 7 light so the detector's `len(rows) // 2` split lands on the transition. **Deliberately not done:** the vault supports neither the −3% threshold nor the 56-day window, every deload trigger it carries is signal-count based or RIR-relative, never a % strength delta, and `halson-2014-training-load-fatigue.md` names *same load, higher RPE* as the dissociation flag to use instead. Moving to corroboration-gating is the correct long-run shape and is a larger change; left for a dedicated pass. Coverage: `test_e1rm_regression_cause.py` (8 cases) + `test_a_deload_is_never_reachable_by_absence` in `test_engine_invariants.py`.

## 2026-08-16: WHOOP sync now resumes from a cursor instead of re-walking full history

**Context.** The 2026-08-12 entry left this open: `sync_cycle` pages through 800+ cycles on every sync and 429s near the end, unnecessary load on the same WHOOP infrastructure whose 502s cause the token-desync reauth bug (2026-08-06, 2026-08-12). `backend/data/logs/api.log` shows the pattern recurring roughly every 1-4 days since 2026-07-23: a 502 during token rotation forces the built-in single retry, which then gets a confirmed-dead 400, requiring manual reauth. The two prior fixes (shutdown race, missing `scope=offline`) are both verified working in the current code; neither addresses sync load itself as a trigger for the WHOOP-side 502s. `sync_recovery`, `sync_sleep`, `sync_workout`, and `sync_cycle` all shared the same `_paginate()` helper with no bound, every scheduled sync (2x/day) walked the entire account from the beginning.

**Mechanism.** WHOOP's v2 collection endpoints accept `start`/`end` query params that filter by each record's own occurrence time (confirmed against `developer.whoop.com`'s API reference), not `created_at`/`updated_at`, this is a genuine resumable-fetch filter, not a change-feed. The obstacle to using it naively: a record can carry a PENDING score at fetch time and only finalize later (a cycle's score isn't final until the cycle ends, up to ~24h after its `start`), so resuming from the exact last-seen occurrence timestamp would permanently miss any record that hadn't finished scoring by the previous sync.

**Decision.** Each of the four paginated sync functions now reads a stored per-endpoint high-water-mark cursor (new `whoop_sync_cursor` table, migration 0097) and fetches from `cursor - 7 days` instead of the start of time. The 7-day overlap is deliberately generous against the ~24h worst-case scoring delay; the re-fetched records are cheap and idempotent (existing `content_hash`-gated upserts already no-op on unchanged rows), so the only cost of the wide margin is a handful of extra rows per sync, not correctness risk. The cursor advances to the newest occurrence timestamp seen in the full fetched page, including any still-unscored record, so that record re-enters the fetch window on the next sync rather than aging out. Considered and rejected: reusing `oauth_state(source=...)` for these cursors, the way `hevy.sync_workouts` already does for its own single cursor. `oauth_state` is enumerated directly as the "connected data sources" list in `/api/oauth/status` and `DailyState.data_sources` (`dashboard.py`, `subject.py`), one row per real external connection; four `whoop_recovery`/`whoop_sleep`/`whoop_workout`/`whoop_cycle` rows there would show up as four fake extra WHOOP connections in that UI. A dedicated table keeps this bookkeeping out of that surface. Each `sync_*` function also takes an optional `force_full: bool = False` kwarg that bypasses the stored cursor for one call (full-history re-walk for a deliberate backfill); `sync_all()`'s orchestration loop is unchanged and always calls with the default, so the scheduled/reauth-triggered path behaves identically except for the bounded fetch.

**Consequences.** First sync (no cursor row yet) is unaffected, full-history walk, same as before. Every sync after that fetches roughly a week's worth of records per endpoint instead of the whole account, which is the actual fix for the 429-near-the-end and the unnecessary WHOOP-side load. Recovery's cursor is keyed off `created_at` rather than a genuine `start` field, recovery records carry no occurrence `start`/`end` of their own (confirmed against the live API), and the docs describe the endpoint's filter as keying off "the associated sleep's timing," which isn't a field the payload exposes directly; `created_at` is the closest available proxy and the 7-day overlap absorbs the slop. Regression coverage in `backend/tests/test_whoop_ingest.py`: cursor read/write round-trip and monotonicity, `_paginate` resending `start` on every page, `sync_cycle` resuming from cursor-minus-overlap, `force_full` bypassing it, first-sync full-walk behavior, and `sync_recovery` advancing its cursor past an unscored record. Unverified: whether 7 days is the right overlap for every record type, since it's a judgment call against undocumented scoring-delay behavior, not a number WHOOP publishes, worth revisiting if a gap ever surfaces in the live data.

## 2026-08-12: WHOOP daily reauth: the refresh request never asked for `offline`

**Context.** Rob: "we lose our whoop auth every day... we need the longest amount of time between auths." The 2026-08-06 entry blamed interrupted rotations and narrowed that race, but flagged itself unverified, and the connection died again on 2026-08-12. Research against WHOOP's own docs found a second, deterministic cause the race theory had masked.

**Mechanism.** WHOOP rotates the refresh token on every use, and its refresh tutorial lists `scope` as a required body parameter: "The `offline` scope allows you to get a new refresh token and an access token." `_refresh()` sent `grant_type`, `refresh_token`, `client_id` and `client_secret`, no `scope`. So each refresh spent the current token and got back an access token with no replacement, after which `tokens["refresh_token"]` raised `KeyError`, the new access token was never stored, and the old refresh token was already dead server-side. The connection then worked for exactly one access-token lifetime (~1h per WHOOP's `expires_in: 3600`) before every later call 401'd and every refresh 400'd. With syncs 12h apart, that surfaces as "it logs out about once a day". This is deterministic, not a race, which is why the 2026-08-06 graceful-shutdown fix could not have stopped it.

**Decision.** Send `scope=offline` on refresh. Store the refresh token BEFORE the access token, it is the half that cannot be re-derived. Treat a missing `refresh_token` in the response as "keep the stored one and log loudly" rather than a `KeyError` mid-rotation. Treat 5xx as `RuntimeError`, not `WHOOPAuthError`: a Cloudflare 502 on the token endpoint leaves rotation state unknown (WHOOP community calls this "token desync"), and reporting it as an auth failure sends Rob through a consent flow over a transient blip. The code exchange now also refuses to store a grant that came back without a refresh token, instead of discovering it an hour later.

**Consequences.** Refresh tokens have no documented expiry, so with rotation now completing correctly the expected time between manual reauths is indefinite, bounded by WHOOP-side revocation, not by our client. Deliberately NOT adding the background refresh cron WHOOP suggests: that recommendation exists to avoid concurrent refreshes, our refreshes are already serialised, and every extra rotation is another chance to lose the token. Regression coverage in `backend/tests/test_whoop_ingest.py` (offline scope sent, missing-token tolerated, 5xx not misreported) because the failure is invisible for an hour and then fatal. Still open: `sync_cycle` pages through 800+ cycles on every sync and 429s near the end, unnecessary load on the same infrastructure whose 502s cause desync.

## 2026-08-10: The sleep history has two eras, and no live code spans the seam

**Context.** Investigating a supplement question turned up what looked like a collapse in sleep quality: REM fell from ~140 min/night (31% of sleep) across 2024 to ~95 min (22%) from 2025-03 onward, sustained 17 months with no recovery, and 2026-08 is the lowest month on record at 19.5%. Read naively against a diagnosed OSA and an off-CPAP status, that is an alarming physiological finding. It is almost certainly not one.

**Mechanism.** Everything that should worsen if the cause were physiological instead improved across the same seam. Disturbances fell 13 -> 9, awake minutes fell ~70 -> ~26, sleep efficiency rose 88% -> 94%, and respiratory rate stayed flat at ~13.6. No sleep pathology halves your awakenings while reshuffling your stage percentages; a re-scoring does. The seam also lands exactly on a known ingest boundary, the WHOOP CSV export ends 2025-02-15 and API sync takes over, and the `source` column reads `whoop` on both sides, so it cannot discriminate. A second table corroborates independently: `whoop_journal`, populated the same day, also terminates at 2025-02-15. Two unrelated tables ending in the same week is a data handoff, not a physiology.

**The separation is total, and the variance moved too.** At monthly resolution the two regimes do not overlap at all: 14 pre-months range 27.3%, 34.7% REM, 18 post-months range 19.7%, 25.5%, so the pre-period's worst month beats the post-period's best. Mean nightly SD also falls from 8.8pp to 5.0pp. That second fact is the stronger evidence, a physiological change shifts the mean but does not usually halve night-to-night dispersion, whereas a smoother, more regularised staging model does both at once. Note that no single *night* separates the regimes: pre-period nightly SD (8.8pp) exceeds the shift itself (~6pp), so a three-week daily window straddling the boundary looks like pure noise and briefly argued against a step change at all. The step is real at month resolution and unresolvable below it.

Ruled out: Lexapro. It restarted 2026-02-06, a year after the step, and REM ticked *up* in the months following. Not ruled out, and not distinguishable with the data on hand: CPAP discontinuation (the date was never recorded, only "confirmed off by 04/2026").

**Decision.** No guard in application code, and no truncation of the pre-2025-03 rows. Every live consumer of `sleep` reads a bounded window: `metrics._sleep` and the consistency calc use 14 days, `self_learning._historical_sleep_metric` uses `_SLEEP_LOOKBACK_DAYS = 180`, and the widest `lab_questions.window_days` is 365. The seam is 528 days back and recedes by one day per day, so no rolling window can reach it again. A boundary constant threaded through code that cannot reach the boundary would be a speculative abstraction.

What *is* exposed is ad-hoc querying, so the fix is placed where ad-hoc queries look: migration 0092 adds `v_sleep_era`, which is `sleep` plus an `ingest_era` column (`csv` before 2025-03-01, `api` after). It shows up in the table listing, which is the first thing anyone, human or agent, reads when opening this database cold. Splitting on it reproduces the finding directly: csv 366 nights at 30.0% REM (SD 8.9), api 526 nights at 22.6% (SD 5.1). The view is deliberately not consumed by any code path; making it load-bearing would give a month-resolution boundary a precision it has not earned.

**Consequences.** `personal_sleep_bands` is clean and stays clean, it fits `disturbance_count` and `sleep_cycle_count`, both of which shifted at the seam, but on a 180-day window that cannot reach 2025-03. The one all-time query, `dashboard.py`'s top-5 longest nights, reads duration only, which the re-staging does not touch. What stays exposed is **ad-hoc analysis**: any full-history query invites exactly the wrong conclusion, and this one nearly produced it. Do not compare sleep-architecture metrics across 2025-03 without saying which era each number came from. If the CPAP stop date ever surfaces, that would make the physiological share of the step testable; until then the honest statement is that the ruler changed and the size of any real change underneath is unknown.

## 2026-08-06: WHOOP reauth kept dying because restarts abandoned in-flight token refreshes

**Context.** Rob: "figure out why it keeps turning off every day." The API log pinpointed one clean break, 2026-08-05 18:00:14, `POST oauth2/token` -> 400, after which every subsequent access attempt 401'd and every refresh attempt 400'd on the same dead token, across 7+ separate calls spanning 17 hours and multiple restarts. Refresh tokens don't self-heal; once dead, every future call fails until the full OAuth consent flow runs again (`whoop.py::_refresh`, raises `WHOOPAuthError`).

**Mechanism.** WHOOP rotates the refresh token on every use, `_refresh()` stores a brand-new `refresh_token` from each successful response (`whoop.py:151-152`), and the old one is invalidated server-side the moment the new one is issued. Two places in this codebase kill the API process without waiting for pending work: `main.py`'s lifespan shutdown called `scheduler.shutdown(wait=False)`, abandoning any in-flight APScheduler job, including the 12-hour `whoop_sync` interval job (`jobs.py:178-185`), the instant shutdown starts; and `dev-restart.sh` used `kill -9`/`pkill -9` with no grace period, which can't be intercepted at all. If a refresh is interrupted after WHOOP has issued+invalidated the old token but before the new one is written to Keychain, the locally stored token is permanently dead. Given `uvicorn --reload` restarts on every file save during active dev, and `dev-restart.sh` runs at the start of most sessions, this is a real, recurring window, not a fixed daily token lifetime, just a coin-flip that happens to land roughly once a day.

**Decision.** `scheduler.shutdown(wait=True)` in `main.py`, in-flight scheduled jobs (like `whoop_sync`) now finish before the process exits. `dev-restart.sh` now sends `SIGTERM` first (to both the port-bound PIDs and any `uvicorn shc` process), waits 2s, and only then falls back to `-9` for anything still alive, closing the manual-sync-interrupted-by-restart window too, with the original hard-kill safety net preserved as a fallback, not removed.

**Consequences.** No behavior change on the happy path, restart time is unchanged when nothing is mid-flight, since the grace-period wait replaces the old flat `sleep 2`. Does not fix a refresh broken by other causes (WHOOP-side session revocation, scope changes), those still need manual reauth via `/auth/whoop/login`. Unverified: whether this fully eliminates the failure mode or just narrows the window, since it's a race, not a deterministic bug, worth watching whether reauth is still needed on the same rough cadence going forward.

## 2026-08-05: The engine writes the numbers; effort gets a block axis

**Context.** Four structural gaps from the 2026-08-05 design review, all "what the engine doesn't decide yet" rather than bugs. (1) The engine output trend calls and ceilings while the planning LLM re-derived actual load×rep fresh every session, the layer where the recurring failures lived (hand-computed Epley ceilings, loads between notches, RPE labels the load can't deliver). (2) Effort had a day axis (`rpe_cap_for`) but no block axis: week 1 and the final accumulation week prescribed identical RPE, so the calendar deload shed nothing predictable. (3) A missed or gated session's sets silently evaporated from the fixed Tue, Fri split. (4) e1RM refresh was incidental: invariant 15 made beating a stale e1RM legal, but nothing ever asked for it, so every ceiling could quietly compound against an old reference.

**Decision.** Four additions, all advisory context, validators unchanged. `training/prescriptor.py` owns double progression: last top set + curated rep window + loadable notch grid + today's effort ceiling -> the exact next load×rep (grind ≥ RPE 9.5 repeats; a capped day holds the window top; a ceiling below the last weight prescribes the snapped ceiling). `meso_rpe_band` ramps the working-set target 7-8 -> 8.5-9.5 across accumulation, clamped by the day cap, and `_effort_overreach` adds the planned midpoint rise to its required rise so following the ramp cannot read as grinding. `remaining_split` reflows `target − current` over the sessions whose weekday is today or later, surfacing unplaceable sets instead of dropping them. `pr_reanchor_due` schedules one deliberate top set on a true high day for a non-regressing lift whose weekly-e1RM peak is ≥ 4 weeks old. Evidence-menu picks additionally carry a `progressibility` annotation (dominant step as % of top notch, same prove-a-gap evidence bar the snapper uses) so a coarse-pitch machine is progressed by reps rather than misread as stalled.

**Why.** Moving arithmetic from the LLM into deterministic, tested code converts validator rejections into copy errors; the ramp makes fatigue arrive on schedule instead of by accident, and the overreach adjustment errs toward NOT deloading (the trigger stays corroboration-gated, and only effort above the plan's own ask counts). Reflow makes the weekly target a controlled quantity; the PR ask closes the loop invariant 15 left open.

**Consequences.** The prescriptor prescribes off the SAME per-hand RIR-adjusted e1RM map as every other surface (invariant 13), a second basis must never be introduced. `effort_band_for` supersedes raw `EFFORT_RPE_BAND` lookups in the context; the static band remains the no-meso fallback and the cap invariant still holds for every week × intensity (tested). Deliberately deferred: performance-based MRV fitting (starved until RPE coverage grows, an inert fit violates invariant 6's own philosophy) and selection trial-slots/catalog depth (blocked on the 77%-unverifiable-citation provenance problem; curation must run from vault notes). Coverage: `test_prescriptor.py` + additions to `test_autoregulation.py`.
